---
title: Understand & track public decisions
description: Tools for examining council meetings, public budgets, representatives' votes and political promises, with attention to what period the records cover.
---

Before proposing a different use of public money or questioning a representative's record, it helps to see the underlying decisions. These tools make government records easier to inspect. Their coverage dates matter: an accessible website can contain a historical dataset.

## Follow council meetings

**[OpenCouncil](https://opencouncil.gr/en/about)** makes meetings of selected Greek municipal councils and committees easier to explore through video, timestamped transcripts, agenda-item summaries and speaker contributions. Choose a supported municipality, meeting and subject, or use its [search](https://opencouncil.gr/en/search) to find discussion of an issue.

It also offers [neighbourhood updates](https://opencouncil.gr/en/notifications) by WhatsApp or SMS, with email as another option. Availability depends on municipality coverage; notification delivery was not tested for this guide.

On the [inspected meeting page](https://opencouncil.gr/zografou/sep21_2026_2/subjects/cmu5lhjw104kb11hx0c294h97), summaries are labelled AI-generated and link to a timestamped transcript and recording. Use these as routes into the record. Check important claims against the recording and signed municipal minutes or decisions before concluding what was formally adopted. Access to a summary is not itself a way to submit a view or change a council decision.

## Search Dutch meeting documents

**[OpenBesluitvorming](https://openbesluitvorming.nl/)** brings together public meeting information from participating Dutch municipalities, provinces and water boards: agendas, documents, motions, amendments and other decision-making records. Search for an issue or local organisation, then examine the meeting context and original document before concluding what was adopted. An agenda item or motion is not necessarily a decision that passed.

The portal warns that imported records may be incomplete, incorrect or out of date. It also warns that material removed locally may remain in other copies for a time. Check dates and important details against the originating authority's records; a missing search result does not prove that nothing was discussed.

For people reusing the data, use the current [API documentation](https://openbesluitvorming.nl/docs/api) and [migration guide](https://openbesluitvorming.nl/docs/migration-guide). The older linked-data interface described in Ontola's 2019 article is not the replacement API's contract; see [the historical and current distinction](/concepts/interoperability-and-standards/#make-meeting-records-reusable).

## Explore city datasets

**[Analyze Boston](https://data.boston.gov/)** is Boston's official open-data hub, with datasets on subjects including budgets, permits and service requests. Start by finding the relevant dataset and checking its dates, geographic scope and field definitions.

The portal also lists **[OpenContext (beta)](https://data.boston.gov/showcase/opencontext-democratizing-the-city-of-boston-s-open-data-currently-in-beta)** for asking questions about those datasets through an AI client. The official listing links connection guidance and a public repository. A connector session and answer accuracy were not tested for this guide. Follow that documentation for current access conditions; do not treat a generated answer as an official decision or assume the tool can submit service requests. See [the infrastructure and authority distinction](/concepts/interoperability-and-standards/#connect-public-data-without-handing-over-decisions).

## Budgets and community proposals

**[Bangkok Budgeting](https://bangkokbudgeting.wevis.info/)**, by WeVis, lets people explore Bangkok's spending and community projects. Its [community-fund guide](https://bangkokbudgeting.wevis.info/communityfund/) explains how ideas move through community forums and proposal preparation to district review and procurement. The website also collects ideas for communities to consider; submitting an idea is one step in that wider process.

## Historical records of representatives and promises

- **[They Work for Us](https://theyworkforus.wevis.info/)** — searchable Thai politician profiles and parliamentary voting records. The homepage labels its data as updated in **March 2023**, so use it for the period covered.
- **[Promise Tracker](https://promisetracker.wevis.info/)** — Thai election pledges and records of actions toward fulfilling them. Its homepage gives a last-update date of **12 April 2023**. It distinguishes stages such as a proposal submitted to parliament, work underway and completion.

These are records to examine alongside their sources and dates. A recorded pledge, a proposed measure and an implemented action are different things to check.

For the civic team behind these projects, see [the WeVis story](/stories/wevis-thailand/). To organise discussion of what should happen next, use [Decide & make sense together](/toolkit/decide-together/).
