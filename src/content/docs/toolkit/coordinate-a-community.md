---
title: Coordinate a group or community
description: Tools that help a community organise itself — set its own rules, roles, and ways of making decisions.
---

Before a group can act together, it has to organise itself: agree on who decides what, how, and by what rules. Most online communities never get this far — they default to one all-powerful admin (what Nathan Schneider calls [implicit feudalism](/concepts/implicit-feudalism/)). These tools help a group do better, by designing its own governance deliberately.

- **[CommunityRule](https://communityrule.info/)** (Media Economies Design Lab) — a simple drag-and-drop web app for designing, editing, and sharing your community's own governance: its norms for decision-making, stewardship, and culture. It ships with templates, so a group can start from a known pattern rather than a blank page — or the default of handing one person all the power.
- **[Governable Spaces MCP](https://bookpower.org/governable-spaces/)** — a public, book-grounded assistant that can diagnose implicit feudalism, find precedents, and compare governance forms such as sortition, federated subsidiarity, and plural voting.

## Document community governance

- **[Governance Cards — research prototype](https://blog.citp.princeton.edu/2026/09/18/making-community-governance-legible-a-semester-with-bonfire/)** — Princeton's CROW project and Bonfire explore concise descriptions of a community's infrastructure, membership and rules. The published examples can help a group think about what to document and what should remain private. As of 21 October 2026, [crowcards.org](https://crowcards.org/) still says “Coming soon”; it is not a released card-building app. The final schema and sharing controls remain work in progress. See [making governance legible](/concepts/modular-politics/#making-governance-legible).

## Build coordination skills

- **[Network Weaving Game](https://www.network-weaver-game.com/)** — a Fito Network reflection tool for people coordinating networks and communities. Its questions cover cultivating networks, collaborating systemically, learning together and embodying wellbeing, with follow-on resources for planning and practice. Use it to identify learning goals; the public pages do not establish a validated score of a network's effectiveness. See [learning across a network](/concepts/learning-across-a-network/).
