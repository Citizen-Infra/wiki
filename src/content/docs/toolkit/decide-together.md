title: Decide & make sense together
description: Tools that help groups deliberate, find common ground, and make sense of many voices at once — including a new wave of AI-assisted platforms.
---

Some of the hardest, most valuable things a community does are deciding together and making sense of many voices at once. These tools help groups deliberate, surface common ground, and turn a sprawling conversation into something everyone can understand.

A new wave of them uses AI to do this at a scale that used to be impossible. A few that stand out:

- **[Thinkscape](https://www.thinkscape.ai/)** (Unanimous AI) — splits a large group into many small "ThinkTank" conversations and uses AI agents to weave them into one coherent deliberation, so even very big groups can talk in real time without losing the depth of a small room.
- **[Urbanist AI](https://urbanistai.com/)** — lets residents reimagine their own street or neighbourhood using image-generation AI: take a photo, picture a greener or better version, draw on it together. A natural fit for participatory budgeting and placemaking.
- **[Assembl](https://www.bluenove.com/en/)** (bluenove) — a "massive collective intelligence" platform that distils wide-ranging debates into legible, creative formats — mind maps, videos, even comic-strip summaries — so people can actually see what a deliberation produced.
- **[Convoca](https://convoca.org/)** — community-listening infrastructure from an MIT Media Lab team that pairs human-led AI [sensemaking](/concepts/ai-sensemaking/) with *relational organizing* and, unusually, tools to **close the loop**: making decisions traceable so residents can see who was heard and what changed. Used by New York City's Department of City Planning for neighbourhood planning in Queens and the Bronx.
- **[Deliberation.io](https://deliberation.io/)** — a platform for structured public dialogue, using AI to help identify common ground across participants' contributions. Its public uses include a Washington, DC listening session on AI policy.
- **[Dembrane](https://www.dembrane.com/)** — records group conversations using participants' phones and synthesises them into shared results, with insights traceable to the original contributions. Participants can check the outputs. Designed for assemblies, workshops and other events with several conversations happening at once; see [Voice Matters in Derry/Londonderry](/run-reports/voice-matters-derry-2025/).
- **[CrowdSmart](https://crowdsmart.ai/)** — its Collective Reasoning Foresight product runs structured sessions in which people and AI agents contribute judgments, explain their reasoning and evaluate each other's ideas. The result combines human and agent contributions; see the questions around [synthetic participation](/concepts/synthetic-participation/).
- **[Policy Synth](https://www.citizens.is/policy-synth/)** — Citizens Foundation's open-source framework for teams of AI agents that research a problem and develop candidate policy solutions. Its workflow returns outputs to people for review, voting and decisions, and can run on its own or within Your Priorities. It requires technical setup; see [AI for participation](/concepts/ai-for-participation/) for safeguards.

Groups also use these platforms to discuss proposals and make decisions:

- **[Swiss National AI Dialogue](https://dialogue.publicai.ch/)** — a public input channel for adults to share their priorities and concerns about AI in Switzerland, linked to the development of Apertus. No account is required, but submissions may be public with a participant's canton; read the [privacy terms](https://dialogue.publicai.ch/swiss-national-ai-dialogue/terms). It offers an optional AI interview and promises an open report, without guaranteeing government action. See [Public AI](/concepts/public-ai/#public-input-on-switzerlands-ai).
- **[DreamCon](https://dreamcon.wevis.info/)** — WeVis's public platform for gathering and structuring ideas about constitutional design in Thailand. Individuals and organisers of group discussions can contribute topics and arguments; see [argument mapping](/concepts/argument-mapping/) and [the WeVis story](/stories/wevis-thailand/).
- **[Chatico (Bogotá)](https://bogota.gov.co/mi-ciudad/gestion-publica/chatico-agente-virtual-de-bogota-se-renueva-con-ia-generativa-2026)** — a municipal chatbot that combines public-service information with campaign-specific participation. Residents can answer questions or submit priorities through messaging channels; see its role in the [2024 development-plan consultation](/run-reports/chatico-bogota-2024/).
- **[Loomio](https://www.loomio.com/)** — a decision-making platform for groups and organisations to discuss, propose, and decide together asynchronously, leaving a clear record they can point back to. Built by a worker co-op.
- **[Decidim](https://decidim.org/)** — a major free, open-source platform for citizen participation, born in Barcelona and now used by cities and organisations worldwide for consultations, participatory budgeting, and more. It powers **Brasil Participativo**, the Brazilian federal government's participation platform, which had reached roughly 1.4 million users by 2024 — a sign these tools can work at national scale. Its annual **DecidimFest** gathers the global community around use cases beyond cities and the role of AI in participation.
- **[Your Priorities](https://www.citizens.is/your-priorities/)** — an open-source engagement platform from Citizens Foundation for collecting ideas, adding separate points for and against them, voting, surveys and participatory budgeting. Available as a hosted service or for self-hosting.
- **[All Our Ideas](https://www.citizens.is/all-our-ideas/)** — a wiki survey: participants choose between two ideas at a time and can add new ideas for others to compare. It builds a ranking of group priorities, useful when the organiser should not decide the entire list of options in advance. Available on its own and within Your Priorities.
- **[Go Vocal](https://www.govocal.com/)** (formerly CitizenLab) — a community-engagement platform that helps local governments gather and make sense of resident input, increasingly with AI assistance. A widely used commercial option in Europe and beyond. Its practitioner webinars document real deployments — Copenhagen engaging 12,000 residents in four months, participatory budgeting in St Louis, comprehensive-plan engagement in Allen, Texas — and methods-mix and engagement-culture playbooks.
- **[Make.org](https://make.org/)** — a European participatory-democracy platform that runs large-scale public consultations and surfaces where many thousands of people actually agree.
- **[Polis](https://pol.is/)** — an open-source tool that maps where a large group agrees and disagrees: people vote agree / disagree / pass on short statements (no reply button, so no pile-ons), and it surfaces the statements that win support *across* opinion clusters — the [uncommon ground](/concepts/uncommon-ground/). The engine behind [vTaiwan](/run-reports/vtaiwan-uber/), and the forerunner of the Community Notes [bridging](/concepts/bridging-and-pro-social-media/) algorithm now used by YouTube, Meta and X.
- **[Sensemaker — Jigsaw](https://medium.com/jigsaw/making-sense-of-large-scale-online-conversations-b153340bda55)** — AI-assisted categorisation and summarisation of large public conversations. It groups contributions into themes for people to review; used alongside Polis in [Bowling Green's planning consultation](/run-reports/bowling-green-what-could-bg-be/). See [AI sensemaking](/concepts/ai-sensemaking/) for the method and its limits.
- **[Society Speaks](https://societyspeaks.io/platform)** — statement-based discussions where participants vote agree, disagree or unsure. It groups voting patterns to show opinion clusters, shared positions and disagreements, helping a community see beyond an overall vote percentage.
- **[Viewpoints XYZ](https://viewpoints.xyz/)** — creates polls built around agree/disagree statements, with AI assistance for drafting questions. Share a link or QR code and view responses in real time; useful for gathering feedback during a meeting or from a community.
- **[Consider.it](https://consider.it/)** — an online forum for feedback on proposals, community ideas and strategy discussions. It visually summarises what participants think and why, so people can explore the community's views alongside their own.
- **[Ethelo](https://ethelo.com/technology/)** — compares possible decision outcomes to look for broad support while taking account of how satisfaction is distributed across participants. Its workflows include budget prioritisation, planning and grant evaluation, where a group needs to weigh several options together.
- **[Cortico](https://cortico.ai/)** — a non-profit (from MIT's Center for Constructive Communication) behind the Local Voices Network. Its "talk, understand, share" method records small-group, story-based conversations and uses AI to surface themes into **voice medleys** and **conversation maps** — see [civic listening](/concepts/civic-listening/).
- **[Remesh](https://www.remesh.ai/)** — an AI-assisted platform that lets a large group "talk" by reacting to each other's statements in real time, revealing where opinion converges. The [UN Department of Political and Peacebuilding Affairs' Innovation Cell](https://webtv.un.org/en/asset/k1v/k1vm6h4kve) used this pattern in digital dialogues connected to Yemen, Libya, and Iraq, with capacity for up to 1,000 participants.
- **[Harmonica](https://harmonica.chat/)** — an AI facilitator for group sensemaking: it holds a structured one-to-one conversation with each participant (on the web or via Telegram), then synthesises everyone's input into a shared summary. Designed to *augment* human facilitators rather than replace them; open-core, with a public open-source edition, optional anonymity, and bring-your-own-model support. Suited to brainstorms, retrospectives, and consultations that can't fit everyone in one live room.
- **[Gemot](https://gemot.dev/)** — an open-source (Apache-2.0) tool where AI *agents* deliberate: they submit positions, vote, and it surfaces the **cruxes** — the specific claims that actually divide them, scored rather than summarised — with anti-sycophancy checks so agents can't be nudged into fake agreement. It can also turn a body of published human positions into agents that deliberate on their behalf, useful for mapping a contested debate, though that shades into the [synthetic-participation](/concepts/synthetic-participation/) question of when a stand-in stops representing a real person.
- **[Facilitating Deliberation MCP](https://bookpower.org/facilitating-deliberation/)** — a private, book-grounded assistant for designing a deliberative process, checking whether the sponsor is ready, choosing activities, and shaping the next session. Access is available by request.

## Connect live discussion, polling and reporting

**[CivicOS](http://bloom-project.org/cohort)** is BLOOM's described open-source platform for its community assemblies. **Table Themer** captures table contributions and displays emerging themes during live discussion; **Open Poll** lets residents submit statements and respond agree, disagree or unsure; public reports bring the material together for residents and decision-makers. These are the project's published feature descriptions, not an independent accuracy test or evidence that officials will act on the output.

BLOOM's [civic-host programme](/toolkit/fund-collective-work/#civic-host-funding-blooms-2027-us-cohort) combines these tools with local organising, facilitation and a route to a real decision. The platform is one part of that process; a poll or generated report cannot supply the host's relationships, mandate or implementation commitments.

## Compare public engagement with AI

The **[PAVE — Public AI Voice and Engagement Case Book](https://pave.pairs.site/)** collects examples of people learning about, discussing and seeking to shape AI. Full cases describe **who took part, what they did and how the process worked**, with links to recruitment approaches, public recommendations, facilitation guides and other materials. It is a way to compare design choices with the needs of a particular community.

Its [methodology](https://pave.pairs.site/about/) distinguishes featured cases, full cases and short mapping entries. The site listed 134 entries on 31 October 2026; that is not a count of equally detailed or independently evaluated processes. Editors combine contributed material and desk research, curate for geographic, methodological and thematic variety, and pay attention to inclusion. The collection is deliberately selective. Read the original project sources before borrowing a method or drawing conclusions about its effects; the [Manchester panel](/run-reports/manchester-peoples-panel-ai/) is one example of the different questions a fuller case can answer.

## Plan and facilitate a conversation

- **[Democracy Beyond Elections handbook](https://www.newdemocracy.com.au/2026/09/14/democracy-beyond-elections-a-handbook-on-citizens-assemblies/)** — a free second-edition reference for people commissioning, planning, designing, delivering or facilitating a citizens' assembly. Start with the introduction and use our [role-based chapter map](/concepts/running-a-citizens-assembly/#reading-the-handbook-by-role) to choose where to read next.
- **[Framing questions](/concepts/framing-questions/)** — a practical guide, drawing on Catherine Barnes's [Toda report](https://toda.org/publications/policy-briefs-and-reports/framing-questions-for-dialogue-and-deliberation/), to choosing prompts for dialogue, deliberation and decisions. Includes phase-specific examples and checks for assumptions, participation and personal disclosure.
- **[NCDD Resource Center](https://www.ncdd.org/rc.html)** — a collection of public-engagement methods, facilitation guides and assessment tools. Its [Beginner's Guide](https://www.ncdd.org/beginners-guide.html) is a starting point for people new to dialogue and deliberation.
- **[NCDD Engagement Streams Framework](https://www.ncdd.org/streams.html)** — comparison charts for choosing an engagement method by its purpose, group size, meeting format and participant selection. See [participatory policymaking](/concepts/participatory-policymaking/) for its four purposes.

To understand what digital tools can and can't do, see [AI for participation](/concepts/ai-for-participation/) and [Can AI scale deliberation?](/concepts/ai-and-deliberation/). For real uses, see [vTaiwan](/run-reports/vtaiwan-uber/), the AI-facilitated [Taiwan Alignment Assemblies](/run-reports/taiwan-alignment-assemblies/), and [zKE's consultations in Kenya](/run-reports/zke-kenya/), which connected digital tools to youth assemblies and radio.
