---
title: Fund collective work
description: Tools and models for raising money for shared, community-owned work — without the funding pulling the work away from its mission.
---

Shared work needs shared resources. These tools help a community raise and manage money for collective work — keeping it transparent, and keeping the core a commons rather than a product. For the bigger picture of *how* commons-based projects get funded, see [Funding the commons](/concepts/funding-the-commons/).

## Raise money together

- **[Goteo](https://www.goteo.org/)** — a crowdfunding platform built specifically for the commons and community-oriented projects, which can add institutional *match-funding* on top of community donations.
- **[Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/)** — transparent collective finances: every contribution and expense is public, and a "fiscal host" handles the legal entity and banking, so a group without its own legal structure can still receive and spend money openly.

## Pool savings with clear rules

**Savings circles** are another model of collective finance, not the same thing as donations to a project. In his [oral history](https://protocol.ecologies.info/interviews/mwaselela-saving_circles/), Tanzanian organiser Alinagwe Mwaselela describes groups that rotate a pooled contribution between members, groups that accumulate savings and lend to members, and groups that pool resources for a shared business. Their purposes and money flows differ; not every circle funds a common project.

Before choosing such a model, clarify who contributes, who can approve payouts or loans, how members inspect records, what happens if someone cannot pay, and how disputes or departures are handled. Mwaselela describes both local trust and losses when records or money went missing. Digital records may change who can see transactions, but a wallet or notification does not guarantee that money is safe or rules are fair.

This is a governance example, not a recommendation to join a specific scheme, take a loan or use a token. Check the arrangement's risks, charges and applicable requirements. His separately described Nyota community-currency pilot is not interchangeable with a savings circle.

## Keep it going

- **[Liberapay](https://liberapay.com/)** — recurring donations to people and projects, run as a non-profit with no cut taken; good for sustaining ongoing work rather than one-off campaigns.

## Decide together how to spend it

- **[CoBudget](https://cobudget.com/)** — lets a community allocate a shared pot of money *collaboratively*: members propose ideas, see the budget, and decide together where it goes. Pairs naturally with participatory budgeting.

## Own it together

- **Community land trusts (CLTs)** — a non-profit holds land in trust for lasting community benefit, taking it permanently out of the speculative market so housing or farmland stays affordable and locally governed. A practical form of [relational property](/concepts/relational-property/).
- **Community-supported agriculture (CSA)** — members pay a local farm up front for a season's share, sharing the risk and keeping the farm viable: patronage and a shared stake instead of pure market exchange.

## Explore models before choosing one

- **[Beautiful Solutions online toolbox](https://beautifultrouble.org/toolbox/bsol)** — freely readable values, principles, questions, solutions and stories for exploring solidarity-economy models. You can start with the [Community Land Trust entry](https://beautifultrouble.org/toolbox/tool/bsol-community-land-trust). An independent [Book Power MCP companion](https://bookpower.org/beautiful-solutions/) searches an 85-entry English snapshot and helps an AI agent compare models or prepare a discussion, keeping each example in its local context.

Two patterns worth knowing as you grow — **capped returns** (investors earn up to a ceiling, then the venture reinvests all surplus in its mission) and **open value accounting** (tracking and rewarding everyone who contributes, not just founders) — are explained in [Funding the commons](/concepts/funding-the-commons/).
