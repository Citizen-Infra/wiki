---
title: "Cynefin: matching how you act to the situation"
description: A sense-making framework for telling ordered, complex and chaotic situations apart, so a group picks a way of deciding that fits, especially in a crisis.
---

Most bad decisions in public life are not stupid; they are the right method used in the wrong situation. A council treats a tangled social problem like a broken pipe that one expert can fix, or keeps consulting while a flood needs someone to act now. **Cynefin** (a Welsh word) is a framework, created by Dave Snowden and developed over 27 years of practice, for asking first *what kind of situation are we in?* and only then *how should we decide?*

## How it works

The EU's Joint Research Centre field guide describes Cynefin as "a decision support framework": a way of choosing a method while critically assessing when to change it. It rests on a principle of **bounded applicability**: there are few if any solutions that work everywhere, but many that are valid in a particular context.

The framework sorts situations by the kind of **constraint** that holds them together:

- **Order**, where constraints are strong enough that outcomes are predictable while they hold. It splits into two:
  - **Clear**: cause and effect are obvious to any reasonable person. Follow good practice.
  - **Complicated**: cause and effect exist, but it takes expertise or analysis to find them. Ask the experts.
- **Complex**, where outcomes are uncertain but the system still has *enabling* constraints: many entangled factors, no single line from cause to effect. You cannot analyse your way to the answer in advance; you run small, safe-to-fail experiments and watch what emerges. The guide notes that most organisations (and most communities) are complex in this sense, because needs entangle with identities, cultures and values.
- **Chaos**, the absence of effective constraint. Act first to create some stability, then work out what is going on.
- **Confused**: not knowing which of these you are in, which is where people often are, and where the temptation is to default to whatever method they already like.

Moving between these states is costly. The guide compares it to latent heat: energy has to go in before the phase changes, which in organisations shows up as the effort of changing how people relate to each other.

## Using it in a crisis

Snowden and Alessandro Rancati's [*Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis*](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC123629) (JRC, 2021), written for EU decision makers, turns the framework into a four-stage path:

1. **Assess** the type of crisis and start a response: set clear, temporary "draconian" boundaries to buy time to think, start a journal from day one, and ask diverse groups to assess the situation independently of each other.
2. **Adapt** to the new pace: manage constraints, break information into smaller fragments, form small specialised crews instead of involving everyone in everything, and build **human sensor networks** that feed fast, distributed signals to decision makers.
3. **Exapt**: radically repurpose existing structures, tools and assets to create something new, and run parallel experiments.
4. **Transcend**: stabilise, manage cadence and control, and ritualise learning while the lessons are still fresh.

Several of these map directly onto citizen infrastructure. A human sensor network is a deliberately diverse group of people who report what they see, and the guide argues it works best when it already exists in normal times, so people are familiar with it before a crisis hits. It also warns that polls and surveys capture surface responses that are easy to game, and that social-media monitoring can become "an unbuffered, easily manipulated feedback loop." Diverse, independent observation is the point, not volume.

## Why it's here

Cynefin is a practical check before choosing a participation method. A [citizens' assembly](/concepts/running-a-citizens-assembly/) is built for complex questions where values and trade-offs are entangled; an expert review suits complicated ones; neither suits chaos. The framework's emphasis on many small, independent observations links it to [civic listening](/concepts/civic-listening/) and [AI sensemaking](/concepts/ai-sensemaking/), and its insistence on keeping a question open in complex situations echoes [productive uncertainty](/concepts/productive-uncertainty/).

The framework is still developing. In October 2026 the Cynefin Company published *Cynefin in the Age of Entanglement*, the first in a planned series of pocket field guides, illustrated and produced by Nia Angharad Williams and edited by Beth Smith. Snowden's launch post stresses that the guide sets out what Cynefin is and also what it is not, to stop the framework "hardening into an -ism", and that the series will link to Cynefin.io, the company's open-source wiki.
