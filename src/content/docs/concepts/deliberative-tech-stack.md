---
title: The deliberative tech stack
description: Six jobs that technology can support in a public discussion, from hearing one another to making a decision, and the foundations they depend on.
---

A **deliberative tech stack** combines tools for different jobs in a public discussion. A group might need to hear people's experiences, frame a question, examine evidence, compare options and decide what to do. Lisa Schirch's *Deliberative Tech Hub* deck separates these jobs into six layers, with supporting work on safety, privacy and who gets to participate.

The point is to choose tools around the group's goals. For example, [zKE in Kenya](/run-reports/zke-kenya/) connected youth assemblies and radio discussions with a WhatsApp bot, Talk to the City for voice contributions, Polis for finding shared proposals, and Remesh to bring the strands together. The stack gives organisers a way to name the different jobs within a process like that.

## The six layers

Schirch's diagram presents the layers in the order below. The examples are selected from her accompanying table; they illustrate particular functions rather than a complete package to install.

| Layer | What the group needs to do | An example named in the deck |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Humanizing** | Establish constructive norms, coach participation and exchange the personal experiences behind people's views. | [Soliya](https://soliya.net/) organises facilitated dialogue across differences. |
| **2. Agenda setting** | Let participants identify, define and frame the issues they want to discuss. | [Polis](https://pol.is/home2) lets people contribute statements and react to others' views. |
| **3. Fact building** | Share information and learning resources, distinguish sources and identify gaps in what the group knows. | [Ponder.wiki](https://ponder.wiki/) is an experimental prototype for collaboratively analysing complex issues and evidence. |
| **4. Sensemaking** | Map agreement and disagreement, uncover values and mistaken assumptions about others' views, and work through reasons and trade-offs. | [Consider.it](https://consider.it/) visually summarises what a community thinks and why. See also [argument mapping](/concepts/argument-mapping/). |
| **5. Weighing scenarios** | Imagine possible futures and compare the consequences and trade-offs of different options. | [Ethelo](https://ethelo.com/technology/) evaluates possible decision outcomes; its workflows include planning and budget prioritisation. |
| **6. Decision-making** | Develop proposals together, choose a voting method or allocate a shared budget. | [Cobudget](https://cobudget.com/) lets groups develop and fund projects together. |

Some tools appear in several places in Schirch's table. Polis supports both agenda setting and opinion mapping; Ethelo appears in sensemaking, scenario comparison and budgeting. Treat the layers as functions to design for, with room to revisit a question as the discussion develops.

## The supporting foundations

Schirch's foundations slide distinguishes **foundational technology** from work on **recruiting and sortition**. Together they address how people can take part and whose voices are heard:

- **Safety:** protect systems and accounts through measures such as threat detection and multi-factor authentication.
- **Identity and data privacy:** decide how identities are verified and contributions protected, using measures such as encryption, anonymisation and consent management.
- **Recruiting participants:** find and contact relevant stakeholders, including through contact-management systems and social media.
- **Recruiting and sortition:** bring people from different identity groups into the discussion and ensure that marginalised voices are heard and acknowledged. [Sortition](/concepts/sortition/) explains how selection by lot can support representation.

These choices need attention alongside the discussion tools. A map of participants' opinions tells an organiser about the people who took part; assessing who is missing requires looking at recruitment and selection too.

## Where the stack can be used

Schirch names seven settings for deliberative technology:

- **[Citizens' assemblies](/concepts/citizens-assembly/):** a broadly representative group deliberates on public questions.
- **Civil-society consultations:** organisations and groups develop shared positions.
- **Participatory budgeting:** community members allocate money to their priorities.
- **Public input into peace processes:** people examine possible agreements and identify what they could accept.
- **[Platform and AI governance](/concepts/democratising-ai/):** people help shape rules for digital platforms and AI systems.
- **Social-movement organising:** members deliberate on goals and tactics.
- **Future planning:** participants describe the community they want to live in and explore how to get there.

The same function can matter in very different settings. A neighbourhood budget and a peace consultation might both need to compare proposals, while requiring different recruitment, privacy arrangements and facilitation.

## Using the stack to design a process

Start by naming the work participants need to do at each layer and how their contributions will carry into the next part of the process. If voice notes are collected in one tool and proposals compared in another, someone must preserve the meaning of those contributions as they move between them. [Connecting the tools](/concepts/interoperability-and-standards/) covers the practical question of making systems work together.

In Davis Smith's *Testing Deliberative Technologies to Identify Optimal Use*, organisers used Deliberation.io and Talk to the City for broad listening, Polis for opinion mapping and ideation, and CrowdSmart for prioritisation and decision-making. Smith recommends combining tools around their distinct functions, with suitable questions and engagement strategies.

For available platforms, see [Decide & make sense together](/toolkit/decide-together/). For the limits of handing deliberative work to AI, see [Can AI scale deliberation?](/concepts/ai-and-deliberation/).
