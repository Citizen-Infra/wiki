---
title: "WeVis: civic tools built around local participation (Thailand)"
description: How a Thai civic-tech group connects public records, community proposal processes and structured constitutional discussion.
---

**WeVis** is a Thai civic-tech group that makes public information easier to examine and develops tools for participation. Its projects range from budget visualisations to structured discussions about constitutional design. The [team's own account](https://wevis.info/datafordemocracy/) describes the work of gathering and organising records so that people can use them in public debate.

## From budget data to a community proposal

[Bangkok Budgeting](https://bangkokbudgeting.wevis.info/) combines spending information with a guide to community projects. The [published process](https://bangkokbudgeting.wevis.info/communityfund/) connects community forums and a working group that prioritises proposals with district-level review, approval and procurement. Ideas submitted on the website are intended to be passed to communities for consideration.

The [People Powered interview](https://www.peoplepowered.org/news-content/wevis-supporting-participatory-democracy-in-thailand-through-locally-developed-digital-tools) explains why this involves more than putting a form online. Residents also use community meetings, and district staff help with detailed proposals. Digital skills, difficult templates and meetings during working hours can still limit who takes part. WeVis adapted its tools around those existing arrangements.

The team's cooperation with the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration is part of that account. Claims that participation increased trust or reduced waste are the interviewee's interpretation; the case does not provide an independent estimate of those effects or identify every project implemented.

## Making recurring discussions usable

**[DreamCon](https://dreamcon.wevis.info/)** provides a public place to gather ideas and organise discussions about constitutional design. Its workflow connects online contributions with facilitated group discussions and structured arguments that could inform future drafting. This is [argument mapping](/concepts/argument-mapping/) with a practical audience: people trying to build on earlier discussions.

In the People Powered interview, WeVis describes experimenting with Thai transcription and summarisation while worrying about context and minority views. The team also describes evolving guidance for human note-takers. These are development choices and concerns, rather than verified evidence of an AI system's accuracy or constitutional influence.

WeVis's [public-record tools](/toolkit/understand-public-decisions/) support another part of participation: examining budgets, votes and promises before discussing what should change. Their dataset dates remain part of the record.
