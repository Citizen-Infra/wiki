---
title: "zKE's first policy consultations (Kenya, 2024–2025)"
description: Young Kenyans combined youth assemblies, radio and digital deliberation to discuss public participation, taxation and the Youth Bill.
---

After Kenya's 2024 youth-led protests, **Build Up**, the youth organisation **Siasa Place** and the radio programme **The Situation Room** worked together on **zKE**: a way for young Kenyans to discuss policy across public meetings, broadcast media and online conversations. This report covers its early consultations, documented in the organisers' bill-specific reports and [Build Up's May 2025 account](https://howtobuildup.medium.com/from-social-media-polarization-to-online-deliberation-a89d1851e135).

## What happened

Build Up describes **four months** of work connecting in-person youth assemblies, known as *barazas*, and radio discussions to several digital tools. A WhatsApp bot provided updates and collected contributions; **Talk to the City** gathered voice opinions; **Polis** identified proposals with broad agreement; and **Remesh** brought the conversation's strands together in a group session. Social media helped recruit people into these spaces. See [Build Up's account](https://howtobuildup.medium.com/from-social-media-polarization-to-online-deliberation-a89d1851e135) and the [zKE project site](https://www.zkevoices.org/).

The organisers published results for several policy questions:

- **Public Participation Bill 2024:** zKE reports **152 people** at youth barazas across Homa Bay, Nairobi, Nakuru, Kitui and Mombasa, and **239 participants** in its digital consultation. Shared proposals included at least **30 days** for public submissions, accessible digital participation and explanations of how public input affected decisions. [Conversation report](https://www.zkevoices.org/conversations/the-public-participation-bill/).
- **Youth Bill 2024:** the report gives a consultation window of **11–19 December 2024** and **564 digital participants**. Proposals included direct youth involvement in governance, digital skills and employment opportunities, and clearer accountability for youth funding. [Conversation report](https://www.zkevoices.org/conversations/the-youth-bill-2024/).
- **Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2024:** the report records a public submission window of **25–28 November 2024**. Participants called for accountability over tax spending and better access to farming equipment and inputs, among other proposals. [Conversation report](https://www.zkevoices.org/conversations/the-tax-amendment-bill-2024/).

These are the organisers' figures for particular activities. They should be kept separate: adding them would not establish how many different people participated across zKE.

## What came out of it

The public outputs include issue summaries and linked Polis results showing shared and divisive positions. For example, the [Public Participation Bill report](https://www.zkevoices.org/conversations/the-public-participation-bill/) distinguishes support for longer submission periods from disagreement over a proposed minimum participation threshold. It also records an intention to forward views to the Attorney General's office. **Receipt, government response and adoption of particular recommendations remain unverified here.**

Participation combined public invitations online with organised local meetings. The published reports do not establish a nationally representative sample, so the findings describe the people who took part. This early cycle also sits within a continuing initiative: the [project site](https://www.zkevoices.org/) presents later conversations alongside these results.

## Why it matters

The case shows how [AI-assisted deliberation](/concepts/ai-and-deliberation/) can connect several forms of participation. Local meetings and radio give people ways into a discussion; digital tools help collect contributions and find shared proposals. The [toolkit](/toolkit/decide-together/) explains platforms used for these jobs. The next test is whether institutions respond to what participants produce.
