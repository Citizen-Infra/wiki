**TL;DR.** In early 2025, Bowling Green and Warren County, Kentucky, ran a month-long online consultation, **"What Could BG Be?"**, for the **BG 2050 Project**. Over 33 days, **7,890 residents** posted ideas and cast **1,034,868 votes** on the open-source platform **Polis**. Google's **Jigsaw** used its **Sensemaker** tool, running on Gemini, to group the ideas into 12 themes and surface agreement and disagreement. The resulting input fed a strategic plan presented to **Warren County Fiscal Court on 30 April 2026**; its nine priorities are a planning agenda, rather than nine completed projects.

## What happened

To plan for regional growth, the **BG 2050 Project** wanted public input at a scale its roughly 100-person volunteer leadership team could not gather on its own. Working with **Innovation Engine**, a Bowling Green strategy firm, and Google's **Jigsaw**, the project ran an open online conversation, "What Could BG Be?", on **Polis** — the open-source platform built by The Computational Democracy Project, where participants submit short statements and vote to agree or disagree with others'.

Over 33 days, from 14 February to 17 March 2025, **7,890 residents** weighed in **1,034,868 times**. The organisers used Jigsaw's **Sensemaker**, built on Google's Gemini, to categorise ideas into **12 high-level topics** and more detailed subtopics, and to write summaries for the report. Sensemaker also separated where residents converged from where they split: by the Jigsaw write-up's account, **2,370 ideas, over half of them, drew agreement rates above 80 percent**. The report names seven areas of strong consensus, among them more family-friendly activities, grocery access, connecting downtown to the riverfront, walkable sidewalks, easing traffic on Scottsville Road, and flood and disaster preparedness.

The organisers are explicit that the AI did not replace human judgement. Jigsaw's account stresses the need for human review: local knowledge helped connect riverfront ideas to investments the county was already weighing. Innovation Engine's post-conversation surveys reported that 70% of participants came away more confident their voice matters and 83% felt they better understood other residents' views, though those figures are self-reported. Jigsaw's June 2026 account also describes use of the data in land-use, parks, riverfront and school work. Those are reported uses of the input, rather than a measure of completed improvements.

## From consultation to a plan

The county-hosted [BG 2050 Strategic Plan](https://www.warrencountyky.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/BG2050-Strategic-Plan-4.30.26.pdf) was presented on **30 April 2026**. Its nine priorities cover:

- a shared regional story;
- attracting new kinds of talent;
- support for innovation and entrepreneurs;
- learning from early childhood through adulthood;
- inclusive career pathways;
- land use and infrastructure;
- quality of life for residents and visitors;
- health and wellness;
- five-year delivery plans.

The plan proposes **one- and three-year milestones within five-year cycles**, with continued community feedback. It establishes the recommendations delivered to county leadership; delivery, funding and results still need to be tracked.

## Why it matters

Most of the AI-and-democracy run reports here use AI to help a recruited or sortition group deliberate. Bowling Green is a different shape: an **open, mass online consultation** where the hard problem is not recruiting a representative few but making sense of input from thousands. That is the gap [civic listening](/concepts/civic-listening/) names, and it is where AI is doing the most practical work right now — [scaling the read side of deliberation](/concepts/scaling-deliberation/) rather than the talk side. The toolchain is a recognisable lineage too: Polis is the same platform behind Taiwan's [vTaiwan](/run-reports/vtaiwan-uber/) and its [Alignment Assemblies](/run-reports/taiwan-alignment-assemblies/) and California's [Engaged California](/run-reports/engaged-california/), now paired with an LLM to do clustering a human team once did by hand. The honest caveat the organisers volunteer — that the AI summaries still needed local people to interpret them — is the part worth carrying forward: the technology made a million votes legible, not self-explanatory.
